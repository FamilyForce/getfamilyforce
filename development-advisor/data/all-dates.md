# All Important Dates — Development Advisor
### Sourced from all 5 FamilyForce playbooks
*Organized chronologically by child's age*

---

## BIRTH — 3 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birth | Safe sleep: always on back, no pillows, no bumpers, no sleep positioners | Sleep | Applies until baby can roll both ways independently |
| Birth | Zero passive screens | Screen Time | Exception: video calls where an adult responds in real time |
| Birth | Background TV off during awake/play time | Screen Time | Background TV reduces parent speech by ~770 words/hour |
| Birth | Breast milk or formula only — no water, no juice, no solids | Feeding | Exclusive milk feeding until 4–6 months |
| 0–3 months | Total sleep needed: 14–17 hours/day | Sleep | Across multiple short sleep periods, no set schedule yet |

---

## 4 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 months | ⚠️ WATCH: Sleep regression begins | Sleep | Permanent change in sleep architecture — more night wakings, normal |
| 4 months | Solids window approaching — start watching for readiness signs | Feeding | Readiness: holds head up, shows interest in food, tongue-thrust reflex fading |
| 4 months | Total sleep needed: 14–16 hours/day across 3–4 naps | Sleep | Night stretch should be lengthening |
| 4 months | Avoid creating new sleep associations during regression | Sleep | Feeding to sleep, rocking — builds habits that need unlearning later |

## 4–6 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4–6 months | ✅ Introduce first solid foods | Feeding | Single-ingredient purees: sweet potato, peas, butternut squash, banana |
| 4–6 months | One new food every 3–4 days | Feeding | Watch for reaction: hives, vomiting, swelling within 20 mins |
| 4–6 months | Texture: smooth puree only | Feeding | No lumps, no chunks at this stage |
| 4–6 months | Breast milk/formula still 90% of nutrition | Feeding | Solids are practice, not meals |
| **4–11 months** | 🚨 CRITICAL: PEANUT INTRODUCTION WINDOW | Feeding | LEAP study: introducing peanuts in this window reduces allergy risk by 80%. Mix 1/4 tsp smooth peanut butter into puree. Introduce 3x/week to maintain tolerance. Window CLOSES at 11 months. Consult pediatrician first if family allergy history. |
| 4–11 months | Begin introducing all 9 major allergens | Feeding | Peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, milk, wheat, soy, fish, shellfish, sesame |

## 5 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 months | Sleep: 14–15 hours/day, 3–4 naps | Sleep | Longest overnight stretch should be reaching 5–6 hours |
| 5 months | Sleep training window approaching — not yet, but prepare | Sleep | Greenlight at 6 months for most methods |

## 6 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6 months** | 🚨 CRITICAL: Sleep training window OPENS | Sleep | Most pediatricians and sleep researchers greenlight at 6 months. Ideal window: 6–9 months. Waiting makes it harder. |
| 6 months | 📅 6-month pediatrician visit — ask about iron levels | Feeding / Health | Iron checks become important as solid food intake increases |
| 6 months | Introduce sippy cup with small amount of water | Feeding | 1–2 oz/day max. Sippy cup is practice, not hydration |
| 6 months | Texture progression: move to mashed and lumpy | Feeding | Smooth puree → mashed → lumpy over next few months |
| **6–9 months** | ⚠️ Introduce ALL 9 major allergens | Feeding | Eggs, tree nuts, fish, wheat, dairy, soy, shellfish, sesame. One at a time, 3–4 days apart. Food diary recommended. |
| 6 months | Sleep: aim for 2 naps, 10–12 hours overnight | Sleep | Sleep training brings most babies here within 2–3 weeks |
| 6 months | Possible second sleep regression | Sleep | Not universal, but common — hold sleep training plan steady |

## 7–9 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7–8 months | Introduce soft finger foods | Feeding | Banana pieces, avocado, well-cooked pasta, soft-cooked vegetables |
| 7–8 months | Pincer grasp developing — let them self-feed | Feeding | Messy is correct. It's how they learn. |
| 7–9 months | Stranger anxiety may begin | Tantrum | Normal developmental phase — doesn't need intervention |
| **8–10 months** | ⚠️ Watch for 3-to-2 nap transition | Sleep | Signs: third nap becomes a fight, bedtime getting harder. Shift gradually — extend wake windows by 15 mins/day |
| 9 months | 📅 9-month pediatrician visit | Health | Language check: should have 3–5 sounds/words |
| 9 months | Continue allergen rotation — confirm all 9 introduced | Feeding | All major allergens should be introduced by 9–11 months |

## 12 MONTHS (1 YEAR)

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12 months** | Switch from formula/breast milk to whole cow's milk | Feeding | Target: 16–24 oz/day. Do NOT use skim or 2% before age 2 — fat needed for brain development. Do NOT give before 12 months. |
| 12 months | No honey before 12 months — botulism risk | Feeding | Applies from birth, enforce actively at this transition |
| 12 months | No juice before 12 months | Feeding | After 12 months: max 4 oz/day of 100% fruit juice |
| 12 months | 📅 12-month pediatrician visit | Health | Vaccines, developmental check, first birthday |
| 12 months | Language: should have ~3 words | Tantrum | Fewer than 3 words at 12 months → mention to pediatrician |
| 12 months | Sleep: 13–14 hours/day total, 2 naps | Sleep | |
| 12 months | Screen time: still zero passive screens | Screen Time | Rule holds through 18–24 months |

## 15 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 months | 📅 15-month pediatrician visit + vaccines | Health | |
| 15 months | Language: should have ~5–10 words | Tantrum | Vocabulary growing — each new word reduces tantrum frequency |
| **15–18 months** | ⚠️ Watch for 2-to-1 nap transition | Sleep | One of the most disruptive sleep transitions. Move nap to midday. Move bedtime earlier during transition (6:30–7pm). Takes 4–6 weeks to stabilize. |

## 18 MONTHS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18 months** | 🚨 TANTRUM PEAK BEGINS | Tantrum | 87% of 18-to-24-month-olds have tantrums (Sisterhen & Wy, 2023). Language gap is at its widest. Prevention is the main tool at this stage. |
| 18 months | 📅 18-month pediatrician visit — language check | Tantrum | Should have ~50 words. Not combining words → ask about speech evaluation |
| 18 months | Language milestone: 50+ words expected | Tantrum | If under 50 words, speech evaluation recommended — language and tantrums are directly connected |
| 18 months | Screen time transition: limited high-quality content now allowed | Screen Time | 18–24 months: AAP allows up to 1 hour/day of HIGH-QUALITY content — co-viewed with parent, slow-paced, educational |
| **18–24 months** | ⚠️ Potty training readiness window OPENING | Potty Training | Watch for signs: staying dry 2+ hours, interest in toilet, hiding to poop. Introduce potty as concept (sit on it clothed first) |
| 18 months | Screen: co-viewing only for any content introduced | Screen Time | Child cannot transfer screen content to real life without parent bridging it |
| 18 months | Sleep: 12–14 hours/day, 1 nap (midday) | Sleep | |

## 24 MONTHS (2 YEARS)

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24 months** | Peak tantrum frequency | Tantrum | Expect daily tantrums, sometimes multiple. Duration: 1–5 min in healthy pattern. Two-phase structure most visible at this age. |
| 24 months | Language: should be combining 2+ words | Tantrum | If NOT combining words → speech evaluation immediately. Late talkers have 1.96x risk of severe tantrums (Manning et al., 2019) |
| 24 months | 📅 2-year pediatrician visit | Health | Developmental screen, language check, molars |
| **24 months** | Screen time limit: max 1 hour/day, high quality only | Screen Time | Quality: Ms. Rachel, Sesame Street, Daniel Tiger. No autoplay. Co-view when possible. |
| 24 months | 2-year molars may be erupting | Health | Increased irritability and drool — check with pediatrician |
| **18–24 months** | Core potty training window | Potty Training | Evidence-based 3-day method most effective here. Clear your calendar. Both caregivers must be aligned. |
| 24 months | Switch to 2% milk from whole milk | Feeding | Whole milk through age 2, then 2% or lower if recommended by pediatrician |

## 2–3 YEARS

| Age | Milestone | Playbook | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 months | Language expanding — tantrums may begin to decrease | Tantrum | Some children begin using "mad" or "no want" before exploding |
| 30 months | Potty training: if not started, window narrowing | Potty Training | Still achievable but more entrenched behaviors to overcome |
| 2–3 years | Screen time habits form permanently | Screen Time | The patterns established now — what they watch, how long, whether parent is present — will be very hard to change at age 5, 6, 7 |
| 2–3 years | Screen Time limit: 1 hour/day max | Screen Time | Below age 5: AAP recommends max 1 hour/day of high-quality content |
| 2–3 years | Potty training: completion window | Potty Training | If not trained by age 3.5, discuss with pediatrician |
| 2–3 years | Annual eye exam — if regularly using screens | Screen Time | Myopia often starts without obvious symptoms |
| 2 hours/day | 🏃 OUTDOOR TIME — every day | Screen Time | Taiwan study: 2 hours/day outdoor light reduces myopia risk significantly. Any outdoor space, any weather. Applies from birth through childhood. |

## RULES THAT APPLY AT ALL AGES

| Rule | Playbook | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Screens off 60 minutes before bed | Screen Time | Not about blue light — about nervous system arousal. Hold this regardless of content. |
| 20-20-20 rule | Screen Time | Every 20 minutes of screen use → look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds |
| Elbow rule for device distance | Screen Time | Bent arm, elbow to fingertip = minimum viewing distance. Child should not be able to touch screen with bent arm. |
| 2 hours outdoor time daily | Screen Time | Any outdoor space, any weather. Prevents myopia. Daylight above 1,000 lux triggers dopamine that tells the eye to stop growing. |
| Annual eye exam once screens start | Screen Time | Myopia often starts without symptoms |
| Background TV off during play | Screen Time | Reduces parent-child speech by ~770 words/hour (Christakis) |
| No autoplay ever | Screen Time | One rule. Takes 10 seconds per platform to enforce. |
| Parent phone face-down during floor time | Screen Time | Broken contingency loop (30 brief interruptions) causes more harm than one long absence |

## THE 9 MAJOR ALLERGENS — Introduction Checklist

*All should be introduced between 4–9 months, one at a time, 3–4 days apart*

| Allergen | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 🥜 Peanuts | 4–11 months | CRITICAL window — see above. 1/4 tsp peanut butter in puree. 3x/week. |
| 🥚 Eggs | 6–9 months | Well-cooked scrambled or pureed |
| 🌳 Tree nuts | 6–9 months | Almond/cashew butter thinned in puree |
| 🐟 Fish | 6–9 months | Pureed salmon or cod |
| 🌾 Wheat | 6–9 months | Well-cooked pasta or bread |
| 🥛 Dairy | 6–9 months | Yogurt or cheese (NOT cow's milk as a drink before 12 months) |
| 🫘 Soy | 6–9 months | Tofu or soy-based formula |
| 🦐 Shellfish | 6–9 months | Pureed shrimp |
| 🫙 Sesame | 6–9 months | Tahini thinned in puree |

## SLEEP SCHEDULE REFERENCE

| Age | Total Sleep | Naps | Night Sleep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 months | 14–17 hrs | 4–5 naps | No schedule yet |
| 4–5 months | 14–16 hrs | 3–4 naps | Longest stretch 5–6 hrs |
| 6–8 months | 14–15 hrs | 3 naps → 2 naps | 10–12 hrs (if sleep trained) |
| 9–11 months | 13–14 hrs | 2 naps | 10–12 hrs |
| 12–17 months | 13–14 hrs | 2 naps → 1 nap | 11–12 hrs |
| 18–23 months | 12–14 hrs | 1 nap (1.5–3 hrs) | 11–12 hrs |
