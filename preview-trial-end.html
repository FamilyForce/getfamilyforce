Still open this month

Tummy time every day

The muscles Emma needs to roll, sit, and eventually walk are built during tummy time. Babies who skip it consistently are slower to hit motor milestones. It only takes 2-3 minutes at a time — but it needs to happen every day.

The move

Lay Emma face-down on a firm surface while she's awake and you're watching. Start with 2 minutes, twice a day. She'll fuss. That's fine. It means she's working.

Introduce a pacifier (if you want one) Closes in 2w

The research on pacifiers is genuinely reassuring. They reduce SIDS risk during sleep and don't cause nipple confusion when breastfeeding is established. But there's a window: after 3-4 weeks, some babies won't take one if they've never had one.

The move

Offer it after a feed when Emma is calm. If she spits it out, try again tomorrow. No forcing, no dipping in sugar. Just offer.

Talk to Emma, not at her

Language development starts now, not at 12 months. Every time Emma coos and you respond, you're building the neural architecture for speech. Babies who hear more conversational back-and-forth at this age have measurably larger vocabularies at age 3.

The move

Narrate everything. 'I'm changing your diaper now. It's cold, I know.' Then pause and wait for her to respond. Even a blink counts. This is a conversation.
