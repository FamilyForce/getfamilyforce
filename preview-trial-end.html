Still open this month

Social smile — spot it and return it

The social smile, appearing around 6 weeks, is the first sign your baby is engaging with the world intentionally — not gas, but a real response to your face and voice. Absent social smiling by 3 months is a clinical red flag.

The move

Get close — babies can only focus clearly at 8–12 inches. Smile, talk, and wait. When they smile at you: smile back, lean in, make it a two-way exchange.

Head control — holds steady when upright

Steady head control by 4 months is one of the foundational gross motor milestones. It signals the neck muscles and upper spine are developing correctly. Absent or significantly delayed head control is one of the first motor red flags pediatricians check for.

The move

Tummy time is the main driver. Hold baby in supported upright positions during waking hours. At the 4-month well-child visit, your pediatrician will assess this directly.

Parent mental health — screen for postpartum depression

Postpartum depression affects 1 in 5 mothers and 1 in 10 fathers. Untreated, it disrupts attachment, language development, and emotional regulation in children. This is a screening window, not a judgment.

The move

At your 6-week OB visit, ask: "Can I do the postpartum depression screen today?" Partners should also screen — paternal PPD is real, underdiagnosed, and treatable.
