Clinical · 3 weeks left

Crawling or alternative mobility

By 9 months most babies are moving — crawling, bottom-shuffling, or rolling to get places. Moving builds the cross-body coordination that feeds language and problem-solving.

What to do this week

Put a toy just out of reach on a firm surface. Tummy time twice a day, 10 minutes each. If no movement by 9.5 months, mention it at your next pediatrician visit.

Screening

Object permanence

Oliver now knows things exist even when he cannot see them. This is a major cognitive leap — the foundation for memory, anticipation, and eventually language.

What to do this week

Hide a toy under a blanket while he watches. Let him find it. Repeat. Peek-a-boo is not just a game — it is practice.

Advisory

Pincer grasp emerging

The shift from palmar to thumb-index grip. Feeds self-feeding, tool use, and eventually writing. Most babies develop this between 9 and 12 months.

What to do this week

Put small soft foods on the tray — peas, banana pieces, small pasta. Let him work for it. Do not help. The struggle is the point.

Advisory

Stranger anxiety peaks

Healthy sign that Oliver has formed strong attachments. He knows who matters to him — and reacts when unfamiliar people get too close.

What to do this week

Do not force contact with unfamiliar people. Stay visible when a stranger approaches. Let him warm up on his terms. Tell grandparents: it is not personal, it is neuroscience.
