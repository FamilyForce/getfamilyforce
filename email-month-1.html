Heads up: 3 windows close this month — meaning the natural developmental timing is ending. These are worth doing first.

This month's priority

Safe sleep setupCloses in 4w

Safe sleep reduces SIDS risk by up to 50%. The setup — firm flat mattress, nothing in the crib, back to sleep — is non-negotiable and needs to be right from night one.

The move

Firm mattress, no bumpers, no loose items. Back to sleep, every time, every caregiver.

Tummy time, starting from birthCloses in 2w

Tummy time builds the neck and shoulder strength that everything else depends on — rolling, sitting, crawling. Babies who skip it fall behind on motor milestones.

The move

Start today: place Noah on his tummy on a firm surface while awake and supervised. Build toward 20 minutes total per day.

Vitamin D drops — start nowCloses in 4w

Breast milk doesn't contain enough vitamin D. Deficiency leads to rickets and impaired bone development.

The move

Get infant vitamin D drops (400 IU). Give one drop daily — easiest to do at the first feeding of the day.

Also worth knowing this month

Skin-to-skin for all caregivers

Skin-to-skin releases oxytocin and regulates your baby's temperature, heart rate, and stress hormones. It builds secure attachment — for both parents, not just the birth parent.

The move

Hold Noah skin-to-skin for at least one session per day, especially in the first few weeks.

Get ready for next month

Social smile — watch for it

Prep: Your baby's first intentional smile is coming — watch for it after a feed when they're content and looking at your face. It will stop you cold.

Swaddle stop — when rolling starts

Prep: As soon as you see rolling attempts, stop swaddling immediately — arms must be free. You don't need to act yet, but know this is coming so you're not caught off guard at 2am.

Newborn hearing screen follow-up

Prep: If your hospital hearing screen came back as a 'refer', a follow-up ABR test should be scheduled within the first month. A refer is not a diagnosis — it's a trigger for a better test.
