This month's priority

Receptive vocabulary — the 50-word checkCloses in 0w

Most parents track how many words their child says — but receptive language (what she understands) is a much earlier warning sign. A child who understands 50+ words but only says 10 is likely fine.

The move

Test informally: "Where's the dog?" "Can you bring me the cup?" Count how many named objects she responds to correctly without a gesture prompt.

Also worth knowing this month

First steps — the real milestone

Walking independently is the headline milestone of this period — but the quality of the walking matters as much as the fact. Flat flexible shoes, open floor space, and uninterrupted practice time are the three things you control.

The move

Clear open floor space and get shoes that are flexible and flat. Stiff-soled shoes slow the process.

Finger foods and pincer grasp

The pincer grasp — picking up small objects with index finger and thumb — is a major fine motor milestone that directly predicts later hand control. Self-feeding practice is the best way to develop it.

The move

Offer pea-sized pieces of soft cooked vegetables, banana, or soft pasta at every meal. Step back and let her do the work.

Get ready for next month

Stacking blocks — 3 high

Prep: Get a set of unit blocks or stackable cups if you don't have them. Leave them accessible for open-ended play — no instruction needed.

First words beyond mama and dada

Prep: Keep naming everything, reading aloud, and responding to all attempts at communication as real words. The vocabulary sprint starts now.

15-month well-child visit

Prep: Book the 15-month visit now if you haven't. Bring a word count — how many words is Mia saying consistently? The pediatrician will ask.
