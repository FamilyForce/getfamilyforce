Getting ready for Baby Chen

5 preparation windows open right now  ·  all close at birth

Hi Mei,

Scout is built for after your baby arrives — covering every developmental milestone through the first three years. But we wanted to be useful before Baby Chen is born too, so below are the four things that are genuinely easier to sort now than with a newborn in the room.

These preparation windows all close at birth. None of them take long — but each one is much harder to do when you're running on no sleep.

All 5 windows close at birth. The preparation below is easiest done now — before the fog sets in.

Before baby arrives

Choose your pediatrician now Closes at birth

Most pediatric practices require a prenatal consult before adding a new patient. If you wait until after birth, your baby's first visit — which must happen within 3–5 days — may be with a stranger.

The move

Call 2–3 practices this week. Ask about their after-hours line, hospital affiliation, and wait times for sick visits. Book the prenatal consult.

Car seat installed and inspected Closes at birth

You cannot leave the hospital without a correctly installed infant car seat — and studies show 4 in 10 car seats are installed incorrectly. A free inspection takes 20 minutes and removes all uncertainty.

The move

Install the seat now, then book a free inspection at your local fire station or hospital. Most do walk-ins.

Safe sleep setup Closes at birth

Safe sleep setup should be ready before you leave the hospital. Firm flat mattress, nothing in the crib, back to sleep every time.

The move

Set up the sleep space now. Firm mattress, no bumpers, no loose bedding. That's the whole setup.

Pack your hospital bag Closes at birth

Most parents wish they'd packed earlier. The bag isn't about having everything — it's about not scrambling at 2am.

The move

Pack the bag this week. Focus on comfort items for labor and the first 24 hours.

Take an infant CPR class Closes at birth

Infant CPR is different from adult CPR — the technique, the compressions, the airway management. It takes two hours to learn and you hope to never use it.

The move

Book a class through your hospital, OB office, or the Red Cross. Most offer them regularly. Do it before your due date.

Get ready for month one

Tummy time — starts at birth

Prep: Get a firm, flat play mat before the baby arrives. Starting tummy time from day one — even 1 minute at a time — builds the neck strength that everything else depends on.

Vitamin D drops (breastfed babies)

Prep: Buy infant vitamin D drops (400 IU) before the baby arrives. Breast milk doesn't contain enough vitamin D — the drops start in the first few days of life.

Skin-to-skin for all caregivers

Prep: Tell your birth team you want immediate skin-to-skin if your baby is healthy — that's the whole plan. Both parents, not just the birth parent. The bonding chemistry is the same.
