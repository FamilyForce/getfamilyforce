This month's priority

2-month well-child visitCloses in 1w

The 2-month visit is a developmental milestone check and the start of the vaccination schedule: DTaP, Hib, PCV15, rotavirus, polio, hepatitis B dose 2. The developmental assessment covers social smile, cooing, head control, and visual tracking.

The move

* Schedule on time — vaccines are timed to the immune system's development

Tummy time — start from birthCloses in 3w

Tummy time builds the neck, shoulder, and back muscles your baby needs for rolling, sitting, crawling, and standing. Also prevents positional flat head (plagiocephaly).

The move

* 2–3 minutes, 2–3 times per day minimum

Cooing — first vowel soundsCloses in 3w

Cooing is the first sign that a baby is experimenting with their voice intentionally. Absent cooing by 2 months is a clinical red flag for hearing or developmental concerns.

The move

* Get close and respond when Arty coos. Coo back, smile, wait for them to go again.

Also worth knowing this month

Tummy time — build to 20 minutes/day

Twenty minutes of tummy time per day by 4 months is the AAP recommendation. This is the foundation for rolling.

The move

* Build gradually: add 1–2 minutes per week

Parent infant CPR training

In the event of a choking incident or respiratory emergency, the minutes before emergency services arrive are critical.

The move

* Take a hands-on infant CPR and first aid class — hospital classes, Red Cross, or local fire stations offer them

Get ready for next month

Babbling — consonant sounds (ba, da, ma)

Prep: Respond to every babble as if it's a word. Copy the sounds back and wait for a response.

Stop swaddling when rolling signs appear

Prep: As soon as you see rolling attempts, stop swaddling immediately — arms must be free.

Sits with support

Prep: Start propping Arty to sit using a Boppy pillow or your hands. Short sessions build the core strength for independent sitting.

Consistent bedtime routine — builds emotional security

Prep: Start a consistent bedtime routine now — bath, feed, song, sleep. Predictability is a form of safety for your baby.
