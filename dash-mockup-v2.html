Introduce a bottle (breastfed babies) 4 weeks left

Around 6–8 weeks is the sweet spot. Before this, babies are still learning to breastfeed. After 3 months, many breastfed babies refuse a bottle entirely — and staying flexible about feeding makes a real difference when you return to work.

The move
Have someone other than the primary breastfeeder offer the bottle. Use a slow-flow nipple. Try when Oliver is hungry but not frantic. Once a day is enough.

Tummy time every day ✓ Done

Tummy time builds the muscles Oliver needs to roll, sit, and eventually walk. 2–3 minutes at a time, every day.

Talk to Oliver, not at him

Language development starts now. Every time Oliver coos and you respond, you're building the neural architecture for speech. Babies who hear more conversational back-and-forth have measurably larger vocabularies at age 3.

The move
Narrate everything. "I'm changing your diaper now. It's cold, I know." Then pause and wait. Even a blink counts. This is a conversation.

Respond to cries promptly

Research consistently shows that prompt, responsive caregiving in the first months builds secure attachment — which predicts better emotional regulation, social skills, and resilience for decades.

The move
You cannot spoil a 2-month-old. Responding quickly and consistently is the job right now. Let the dishes wait.
