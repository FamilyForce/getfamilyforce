21 Month Milestones

21 Month Milestones — Pronouns Are Starting and Sentences Are Coming

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Twenty-one months brings the first pronouns — 'I', 'me', 'mine' — and longer phrases. Here's what's developing and what to expect over the next few months.

Pronouns — A Bigger Cognitive Leap Than It Looks

Pronoun use — "I," "me," "mine," "you" — typically begins emerging between 21 and 27 months. It looks simple, but it's cognitively demanding: "I" changes depending on who is speaking, which requires understanding that different people have different perspectives. This is an early form of theory of mind.

Most toddlers use their own name in place of "I" for a while ("Lily do it," "Lily's cup") before the shift to first-person pronouns. This is normal. Persistent use of the child's own name in place of "I" past 30 months is a flag worth noting at the 30-month visit.

Language at 21 Months

Most toddlers have 40–60+ words at 21 months with two-word combinations well established. The target is 50 words by 24 months — most toddlers on a typical trajectory are at or near this already. Three-word combinations ("daddy come here," "more apple please") may be starting to emerge.

Keep using expansion in conversations. Reading aloud every day remains the single highest-impact language activity available at this age. The research is clear: children who are read to daily have significantly larger vocabularies at school entry.

Motor Milestones at 21 Months

  • Running and changing direction — Smoother coordination. Rough-and-tumble play is increasing.
  • Stair climbing — alternating feet beginning — Most toddlers go up stairs one foot at a time at 21 months. Alternating feet (one foot per step) develops closer to 2–3 years.
  • Kicking and throwing — Both well established. Catching is still hard — the visual tracking and anticipatory motor control needed for catching develops around 3 years.
  • Copying a vertical line — Imitating a straight line drawn on paper. Circles are harder and typically don't appear until 24–27 months.

Social Play — Parallel Moving Toward Cooperative

At 21 months, parallel play (playing alongside other children) is still the dominant mode, but the first episodes of cooperative play are beginning — brief exchanges, sharing an object, a moment of shared laughter over a game. This development accelerates toward 24–36 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 21-month-old be hitting?

Running and changing direction, climbing stairs with support, 40–60+ words, two-word combinations well established, beginning pronouns (or own name in place of I), three-word phrases starting, parallel play with brief cooperative moments.

When do toddlers start using pronouns?

Pronoun use (I, me, mine, you) typically begins between 21 and 27 months. Most toddlers use their own name before shifting to first-person pronouns. Persistent use of own name instead of 'I' past 30 months is worth noting at the 30-month visit.

How many words should a 21-month-old have?

Typically 40–60+ words. The target is 50 words by 24 months. Two-word combinations should be established. Three-word phrases may be starting.
