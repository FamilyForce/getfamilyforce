13 Month Milestones

13 Month Milestones — Walking, Whole Milk, and Ditching the Formula

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·6 min read

Your baby is now officially a toddler. Walking is consolidating, formula should be gone, and the vocabulary is starting to build. Here's what to focus on in month 13.

Motor Milestones at 13 Months

Walking is establishing — most toddlers who took first steps around 12 months are now walking as their primary mode of getting around. Falls are frequent and normal. Walking style varies enormously in the first months of walking: wide-stance, wobbling, tiptoe walking, and arms held out for balance are all normal through 18 months.

  • Walking as primary locomotion — Crawling is fading. Walking, even if wobbly, is becoming the default.
  • Stair climbing with support — beginning — Pulling up stairs on hands and knees, or one step at a time with your hand. Going down takes longer to develop.
  • Stooping and returning — Crouching to pick up an object and standing back up without falling. This requires significant balance and coordination.
  • Stacking 2 blocks — Should be emerging. Stacking 2–3 blocks is a standard fine motor checkpoint at the 15-month visit.

⚠️ Not taking independent steps by 13 months

Don't wait for the 15-month visit. Mention it at your next available appointment. The expected milestone is 15 months per CDC 2022 guidance, but early flagging gives more time for intervention if needed.

Nutrition — Off Formula, On Whole Milk

If you haven't switched from formula to whole cow's milk yet, do it now. Formula is no longer necessary after 12 months for most children and is significantly more expensive. Whole milk (not low-fat or skim) provides the fat needed for brain development. About 16–24 oz per day is right — enough for nutrition, not so much it displaces solid food calories.

If your 13-month-old is still on formula: switch to whole milk this week unless your doctor gave specific medical instructions otherwise.

Language at 13 Months

First words beyond mama/dada are appearing or imminent. At 13 months, most toddlers have 1–5 words with specific meaning. The target is 10 words by 15–18 months — the milestone that triggers the vocabulary spurt. Every word you label in daily life is a word that arrives sooner.

⚠️ No words at all by 13 months

Raise it with your pediatrician before the 15-month visit. Early speech language referral at this stage is highly effective — waiting until 18 months costs time that matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 13-month-old be hitting?

Walking as primary locomotion, stair climbing beginning, stacking 2 blocks, 1–5 first words, cup use at meals, whole milk established. Not walking by 13 months: mention at your next visit.

When should I stop formula?

At 12 months. Formula is no longer necessary after 12 months for most children. Switch to whole milk (not low-fat) this week if you haven't already. 16–24 oz per day is the right range.

Is tiptoe walking normal at 13 months?

Yes — walking style is highly variable in the first months. Wide stance, wobbling, and occasional tiptoe walking are all normal through 18 months. Persistent exclusive tiptoe walking beyond 18 months is worth mentioning at the 18-month visit.
