36 Month Milestones

36 Month Milestones — The Three-Year Checkup and What Three Years Adds Up To

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Three years is the finish line of the most intensively monitored developmental period in human life. Here's what the 36-month well-child visit assesses, what the flags are, and what happens next.

The 36-Month Well-Child Visit

The 36-month visit marks the end of the most intensive developmental surveillance period and the transition to annual well-child visits. It's the most comprehensive assessment since the 18-month visit.

What will be assessed

Language: 4+ word sentences (flag if mostly 2-word phrases), speech intelligibility (~75% to strangers), color naming (4–6 colors), counting with one-to-one correspondence.
Social: Cooperative play, pretend play complexity, friendships, following instructions in a group.
Motor: Running, jumping, pedaling, beginning to hop on one foot, drawing shapes.
Self-care: Potty training, dressing, feeding.
Behavior: Tantrum trajectory, sleep, emotional regulation, attention span.
Preschool readiness: Separation ability, group instruction-following, self-regulation.

Vaccines at 36 months

DTaP (dose 5), MMR (dose 2), varicella (dose 2), IPV (dose 4), influenza (annual). This is the last major vaccine cluster until 11–12 years.

⚠️ Language flag at the 36-month visit

Speech still mostly in 2-word phrases warrants speech language evaluation. Strangers understanding less than 50% of what your 3-year-old says also warrants a referral. Speech therapy at age 3 is highly effective — the window for early intervention is still open, but it closes faster with every month of delay.

What Three Years Looks Like — the Full Arc

At 36 months, your child:

  • Has traveled from zero words to 4+ word sentences in 36 months
  • Can run, jump, climb, pedal, and is beginning to hop
  • Has real friendships with specific preferred children
  • Plays in sophisticated pretend scenarios that span multiple exchanges
  • Understands concepts like color, number, size, and time (yesterday, tomorrow)
  • Is daytime potty trained or nearly so
  • Can follow three-step instructions
  • Has the emotional regulation to manage most daily frustrations without a full meltdown

None of this happened because you bought the right toys. It happened because of 36 months of serve-and-return conversations, shared meals, consistent bedtimes, responsive parenting, and showing up. The research on what drives early childhood development is clear: you were the primary intervention.

What Comes Next

Annual well-child visits from 4 years onward. Preschool and kindergarten readiness. Continued language development through age 7–8. Reading foundations beginning around age 4–5. The first 36 months are the foundation. Everything built on it will reflect the quality of what's underneath.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is assessed at the 36-month well-child visit?

Full language assessment (4+ word sentences, 75% intelligibility, color naming, counting), social play, pretend play, motor milestones (running/jumping/pedaling), self-care (potty training, dressing), behavior, emotional regulation, and preschool readiness. Vaccines: DTaP, MMR, varicella, IPV, influenza.

How many words should a 3-year-old be able to say in a sentence?

Four or more words per sentence is the 36-month target. Grammar doesn't have to be perfect — irregular verbs and plurals are still developing through age 5. If your 3-year-old is still primarily using 2-word phrases, request a speech language evaluation at this visit.

What are the signs a 3-year-old is ready for preschool?

Following 2–3 step instructions, briefly separating from parents, basic self-care (toileting, washing hands), using words to express needs and frustrations, ability to take turns in a small group. The 36-month visit is specifically designed to assess preschool readiness.
