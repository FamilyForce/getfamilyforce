28 Month Milestones

28 Month Milestones — Potty Training Progress and Fine Motor Precision

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Twenty-eight months is often when potty training hits its stride — or its wall. Fine motor precision is also improving significantly, and sentences are getting longer and more complex.

Potty Training at 28 Months

Most children begin showing potty training readiness between 18 and 24 months, with training typically starting sometime between 22 and 30 months. At 28 months, if training hasn't started yet and readiness signs are clearly present, start now. If training is underway, you're likely past the initial intensive phase and into the consolidation phase — consistent daytime dryness is the goal.

Daytime accidents during consolidation are normal and expected. Accidents don't mean training has failed. Regression after illness, transitions, or new siblings is common and usually self-correcting.

If potty training is stalling

Review the readiness checklist. If readiness signs are clearly present but training is not progressing after 4–6 weeks of consistent effort, try a fresh three-day intensive approach: complete focus on potty awareness, no diapers at home during waking hours, high fluid intake, and immediate reinforcement for any success. The intensity of the three-day method accelerates the association-forming process.

Fine Motor at 28 Months

  • Drawing circles and lines — Intentional, relatively controlled. A recognizable circle (closed shape) is typically emerging at this age.
  • Turning pages in a book — One at a time. This requires fine motor control that wasn't there at 12 months.
  • Unscrewing lids — And rearranging the contents of every container they can find. A phase that requires childproofing everything at counter level.
  • Snipping with scissors — With child-safety scissors, beginning snipping motions. Not precise cutting yet.

Language at 28 Months

Sentences at 28 months are typically 3–4 words and growing. Questions are increasing — "what's that?", "where daddy go?", "why?" The "why" phase, which peaks around 2.5–3 years, is not a behavior problem. It's evidence of developing causal reasoning — the child is genuinely trying to understand how things work. Answer straightforwardly and briefly.

Strangers should now understand most of what your 28-month-old says. If adults outside the family frequently cannot understand them, flag it at the 30-month visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 28-month-old be hitting?

Potty training in progress or consolidating, 3–4 word sentences, 300–500+ words, drawing circles, fine motor precision improving, asking questions (why/what/where), strangers understanding most of what they say.

What is the three-day potty training method?

Three consecutive days of complete focus: no diapers at home during waking hours, high fluid intake to increase bathroom opportunities, immediate enthusiastic reinforcement for any success, calm neutral response to accidents. The intensity accelerates the association-forming process. Most children who are developmentally ready respond well.

Why does my toddler ask 'why' constantly?

The 'why' phase, which peaks around 2.5–3 years, is evidence of developing causal reasoning — your toddler is genuinely trying to understand how things work. Answer briefly and honestly. Saying 'because it just is' or ignoring the question is a missed opportunity. Brief, honest answers satisfy the curiosity and build the habit of inquiry.
