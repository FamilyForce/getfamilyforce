# 35 Months: The Final Push Before the Three-Year Checkup

The 3-year checkup is one month out. Language is fluent, social skills are robust, and your child is ready for the final developmental check before school. Month 35 is when you take a final inventory.

---

Month 35 is the calm before the final comprehensive developmental assessment before your child goes to school. The 36-month well-child visit is a big one. It checks all the boxes: language, social-emotional, motor, cognitive, and school readiness. Your child's development between birth and three years is now largely complete. Your child is no longer a toddler. They are a preschooler.

Use this month to ensure all the foundational skills are solid. If there's anything you've been quietly wondering about, this is your last designated chance to bring it up before your child enters the formal school system.

## Language Milestones at 35 Months

- Full sentences (4+ words) fluent and consistent — communicating complex ideas, asking questions, describing events
- Tells simple stories and describes recent events clearly — narrative structure becoming more coherent; sequence, detail, characters
- 75% speech clarity to strangers — the 36-month clinical benchmark; if strangers are still regularly asking you to interpret, note it for the visit
- Understands complex multi-step instructions — "Go to your room, get your red ball, and put it in the toy box"
- Counts objects 1–5 reliably — the 3-year target; pushing toward 10 is next
- Knows full first name and age — and often last name; practical safety skill

### Speech still largely 2-word phrases at 35 months

If your child is still primarily communicating in 2-word phrases, or if their speech is consistently unclear to family members, this is a significant language delay at 35 months. Call your pediatrician this week. The 36-month visit will almost certainly generate a speech referral, but starting the process four weeks early can save critical time.

## Social/Emotional: School Readiness

With First Son, I assumed school readiness was automatic at age 3. He'd been in daycare, he knew his shapes, he could count. I didn't think of social-emotional readiness as a skill that needed to be built. His first months of preschool were harder than I expected — not academically, but socially. He didn't know how to follow multi-step classroom instructions. He had difficulty waiting his turn. He struggled to separate on difficult mornings. None of that was unusual. But there was more friction than there needed to be.

With Second Son, I understood that school readiness was primarily about: can he follow complex instructions, can he express what he needs, can he wait, can he repair a conflict with a peer. I'd been building those skills deliberately — not through worksheets, through the way I structured daily interactions. "Can you get your shoes and wait by the door?" "Tell me what you need using your words." "Let's think about how your friend felt." By 35 months, he was doing most of those things without prompting.

The 36-month visit will look at all of this. But the visit doesn't create the readiness. The previous 35 months did.

The social skills your child has developed over the last three years are now converging toward school readiness:

- Cooperative play with shared rules and negotiation — playing effectively with peers, taking turns, resolving minor conflicts
- Sharing voluntarily and understanding fairness — the "mine" phase is mostly over
- Separates easily from primary caregiver — secure attachment means they can explore confidently
- Labels own emotions and others' — "I'm angry," "you look sad"; the emotional vocabulary aids self-regulation

## Motor at 35 Months

- Hops on one foot for 2–3 steps — sustained hopping is developing
- Climbs playground equipment with confidence — ladders, slides, monkey bars with assistance
- Draws a person with 2–4 body parts — a head and a body, maybe arms; a recognizable attempt at representation
- Dresses and undresses with minimal help — manages zippers, snaps, buttons with increasing independence

## What to Do Right Now

1. **Review all prior developmental observations.** Compile any lingering concerns for the 36-month visit. If you've been quietly wondering about something, now is the time to bring it.
2. **Practice narrative language.** "What did you do at school today?" "Tell me about your favorite part." Listen for sequence and detail.
3. **Final check of the 36-month visit booking.** If it's not on the calendar, schedule it this week. This is a critical checkup before kindergarten.

The 36-month visit is the culmination of three years of growth. You've built the foundation. Now you're ready for the next stage.
