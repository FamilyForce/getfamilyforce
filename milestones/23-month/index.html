23 Month Milestones

23 Month Milestones — One Month From the Two-Year Checkup

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

The two-year visit is one month away. Here's the complete checklist of what the pediatrician will assess, what the flags are, and what to bring.

Prepare for the 24-Month Visit

The two-year well-child visit includes the second M-CHAT autism screening and is the most comprehensive developmental assessment since the 18-month visit. Here's what will be assessed:

  • Vocabulary: 50+ words — Write them down before the visit
  • Two-word combinations — "more milk," "daddy go," "big dog"
  • Social play — Interest in other children, parallel and beginning cooperative play
  • Pretend play — Multi-step scenarios established
  • Pointing and joint attention — Should be well established
  • Following two-step instructions — "Get your cup and bring it to me" without gestures
  • Identifying body parts — 5+ on request

Language at 23 Months

Most toddlers have 50–100+ words at 23 months. If your toddler is significantly below 50 words and has no two-word combinations, raise it at the 24-month visit. Speech language evaluation at this age is highly effective when started early.

Three-word combinations ("I want milk," "daddy come here") may be emerging. These typically solidify between 24 and 30 months — they're a primary checkpoint at the 30-month well-child visit.

⚠️ Below 50 words at 23 months

This is the flag to bring to the 24-month visit. Early intervention at age 2 has strong evidence of effectiveness. Don't wait to see if it resolves on its own — the 24-month visit is the right time to raise it.

Crib to Bed — Don't Rush It

The crib-to-toddler-bed transition is one of the most common causes of sleep regression in the second year. Most sleep experts recommend keeping children in a crib until 3 years old unless there's a safety reason (consistent climbing out). The crib is a sleep boundary — remove it too early and you spend months managing a child who can roam freely.

If your toddler is climbing out: lower the mattress first, use a sleep sack to limit leg range, then transition if climbing continues. Don't transition just because it feels like time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 23-month-old be hitting?

50–100+ words, two-word combinations well established, three-word phrases beginning, multi-step pretend play, following two-step instructions, identifying 5+ body parts, strong pretend play, beginning cooperative play.

When should I move my toddler from a crib to a bed?

Most sleep experts recommend keeping toddlers in a crib until 3 years old unless they're consistently climbing out. Early transition is one of the most common causes of sleep regression in the second year. If climbing out: lower mattress, use a sleep sack, then transition as a last resort.

What should I bring to the 24-month well-child visit?

A word count (written down before the visit), examples of two-word combinations, observations about pretend play, how they interact with other children, whether they follow two-step instructions, and any concerns about language, behavior, or social development.
