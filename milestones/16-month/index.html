16 Month Milestones

16 Month Milestones — Tantrums Are Starting and That's Normal

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·6 min read

The first real tantrums typically arrive around 15–18 months. They're not behavioral. They're neurological. Here's why, what helps, and what's developing beyond the tantrums.

Tantrums — Why They Start Now

Tantrums peak between 18 months and 3 years, but the first significant ones often arrive around 15–16 months. The cause is not defiance or manipulation. It's neurology.

At 16 months, the prefrontal cortex — the part of the brain that governs emotional regulation — barely exists. Your toddler is experiencing emotions of full adult intensity with essentially no ability to regulate them. A tantrum is what happens when a feeling exceeds the nervous system's capacity to manage it.

What actually works during a tantrum

Stay physically present and stay calm. You are a co-regulator — your calm nervous system helps regulate theirs. Say little. Don't negotiate during the tantrum (it teaches that tantrums work). Don't leave the room. After the tantrum ends: name what happened and reconnect. "You got really upset when we had to leave the park. That was hard."

Prevent where possible: tantrums spike with hunger, tiredness, and transitions. Managing the environment prevents more tantrums than any in-the-moment response.

Parents who stay calm during tantrums have children who recover faster and tantrum less frequently over time. That's not a parenting opinion — it's consistent in the research.

Motor Milestones at 16 Months

  • Running — beginning — The first "run" looks more like a fast stiff-legged walk. Smooth rhythmic running won't arrive for another year. But they're moving faster than walking.
  • Stacking 2–3 blocks — If this isn't happening by 16 months, flag it at the next visit. It's a fine motor checkpoint.
  • Carrying and using tools — Holding a spoon and attempting to self-feed with it. Accuracy is poor but the attempt is the milestone.

Language at 16 Months

Most toddlers have 5–15 words at 16 months. The vocabulary spurt — a period of rapid vocabulary growth — typically begins after the 10-word milestone. Once it starts, new words often appear daily. Keep labeling and expanding everything you can.

Pointing to share interest should be well established. If not, flag it at the 18-month M-CHAT visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do toddlers have tantrums at 16 months?

Tantrums at 16 months are neurological, not behavioral. The prefrontal cortex (emotional regulation) is barely developed at this age. Your toddler is experiencing full-intensity emotions with no capacity to manage them. Stay calm, stay present, say little during the tantrum, reconnect afterward.

What milestones should a 16-month-old be hitting?

Walking confidently, beginning to run, stacking 2–3 blocks, 5–15 words, pointing established, beginning pretend play, self-feeding attempts with a spoon, cup fully established.

When do toddler tantrums peak?

Tantrums peak between 18 months and 3 years. The intensity varies by temperament. Parents who stay calm during tantrums have children who recover faster — the calm response is the most effective tool available.
