35 Month Milestones

35 Month Milestones — One Month From the Three-Year Checkup

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

One month from age 3 — the most comprehensive developmental checkpoint since infancy. Here's the full assessment checklist and what's developing at 35 months.

The 36-Month Visit Checklist — Final Preparation

The 36-month visit marks the end of the most intensive developmental surveillance period. After this, annual well-child visits replace the more frequent schedule of the first three years. Prepare now:

Language to observe before the visit

  • Is your child regularly speaking in 4+ word sentences?
  • Do unfamiliar adults understand roughly 75% of what they say?
  • Can they name at least 4–6 colors?
  • Can they count 3+ objects with one-to-one correspondence?
  • Can they tell a simple story about something that happened?

Social and behavioral to observe

  • Does your child engage in cooperative play with other children?
  • Do they have named friends they ask to see?
  • Are tantrums declining in frequency and intensity?
  • Can they follow three-step instructions?

Motor to observe

  • Running, jumping, and climbing with confidence?
  • Can they pedal a tricycle (if given opportunity)?
  • Beginning to hop on one foot?
  • Drawing shapes — circles, attempts at a person?

Self-care

  • Daytime potty training complete or nearly so?
  • Beginning to dress and undress independently?
  • Using fork and spoon reliably?

⚠️ Flags to raise at the 36-month visit

Speech still mostly 2-word phrases · Strangers understand less than half of what they say · No cooperative play · Cannot follow two-step instructions · No color naming · Frequent intense tantrums with self-harm or breath-holding · No pretend play · Not potty training at all despite readiness signs present for months.

What's at Stake — Preschool Readiness

Three years marks the beginning of the preschool years in most countries. What a child brings to preschool — vocabulary size, ability to follow group instructions, social comfort with peers, self-regulation skills — significantly predicts their early academic experience. This is not about pressure. It's about recognizing that the work you've done in the first 36 months shows up in the classroom.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 35-month-old be hitting?

4+ word sentences, 70–75% intelligibility, color naming (4–6 colors), counting 3+ objects, cooperative play, named friendships, pedaling, beginning to hop, drawing shapes, potty trained, beginning to dress independently.

What are preschool readiness milestones?

The key skills for preschool readiness: following 2–3 step instructions, ability to separate from parents briefly, basic self-care (toileting, washing hands, feeding), using words to express needs and frustrations, and ability to take turns in a small group. These are what the 36-month visit previews.

What if my child isn't meeting milestones at 35 months?

Bring every concern to the 36-month visit next month — that's exactly what the visit is for. Language delays, motor delays, and behavioral concerns identified at 3 years are all highly treatable with early intervention. The 36-month visit is the checkpoint designed for this.
