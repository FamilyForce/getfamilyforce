3 Month Milestones

3 Month Baby Milestones — Rolling Is Coming and the Swaddle Has to Go

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·8 min read

Three months is when early rolling signs start — and when rolling signs start, the swaddle has to stop, immediately and completely. Most parents don't know this. Here's the safety rule, plus the full 3-month development picture and what's coming in month four.

Month three is a transition month. Your baby is becoming more interactive, more social, more engaged with the world. The early survival mode of the newborn phase is ending. And with that comes a batch of new things to pay attention to: rolling precursors, swaddle safety, head control, and the sleep disruption that's about to arrive.

The Swaddle Safety Rule Most Parents Miss

This is the most important thing in this article. Read it before anything else.

Stop swaddling the moment your baby shows any signs of attempting to roll. Not when they complete a roll. When they attempt it.

Rolling signs include: rocking onto one shoulder during tummy time, twisting their hips, getting a knee under their body, consistently trying to turn sideways in the crib. These signs typically appear between 2 and 4 months — some babies as early as 6–8 weeks.

🚨 Safety — this is not gradual

A swaddled baby who rolls face-down cannot push themselves back up or turn their face away from the mattress. This is a suffocation risk. The AAP is unambiguous: stop swaddling when rolling signs appear. Not a gradual transition — the day you see a rolling attempt, stop that night. Cold turkey.

If your baby is already rolling and still being swaddled: stop tonight.

What to use instead of a swaddle

A wearable sleep sack (arms-free) gives warmth without the arm restriction. The transition is often harder for parents than for the baby — most babies adapt within a few nights. Use the same sleep routine, the same environment, and give it at least 3–5 nights before judging.

Motor Milestones at 3 Months

  • Head control — developing — When held upright and supported at the torso, most 3-month-olds hold their head up for several seconds. Some wobble is completely normal at 3 months. Steady head control (no collapsing) is expected by 4 months.
  • Pushes up during tummy time — Lifting the head 45–90° during tummy time, possibly lifting the chest slightly. The arms are starting to bear weight.
  • Early rolling precursors — Rocking to one side, getting a shoulder off the mat during tummy time. True front-to-back rolling typically comes between 3 and 5 months, back-to-front at 5–6 months.
  • Opening hands and swiping at objects — The tight newborn fist is opening. Your baby will begin to swipe at hanging objects and briefly hold a rattle placed in their hand.

Tummy time — keep building

The AAP recommends 20 minutes of tummy time per day by 4 months, spread across the day. At 3 months, you should be working toward this. Tummy time is the primary driver of rolling, and rolling is the prerequisite for all gross motor milestones that follow. If your baby resists tummy time on the floor, use your chest while reclined — it counts.

Social and Language Milestones at 3 Months

Social smile — now consistent

By 3 months, the social smile should be reliable and easy to elicit. If you're not seeing a consistent social smile by 3 months, bring it up immediately at the next visit. This is one of the earliest behavioral screening indicators for autism spectrum disorder, and early intervention is significantly more effective when started before 6 months.

Cooing and early "conversation"

Your baby is now producing longer strings of vowel sounds and varying their pitch and volume to communicate. The back-and-forth is becoming more sustained: you talk, they respond, you pause, they go again. This is not cute noise — it's active language learning. Respond to everything. Name objects in the environment. The quantity of words your baby hears in the first year directly predicts vocabulary size at age 3.

Recognizing familiar faces and voices

Your baby now clearly prefers familiar faces and voices. They'll turn toward the sound of your voice across the room. This is social cognition developing — the beginning of attachment and the groundwork for all social learning.

The 4-Month Sleep Regression — It's Coming

The 4-month sleep regression typically hits between 3.5 and 4.5 months. Most parents are caught completely off guard — the baby who was sleeping reasonably well suddenly starts waking every 45–60 minutes all night.

What's happening: around 3.5–4.5 months, your baby's sleep architecture permanently matures from primarily deep sleep to the adult pattern of cycling between light and deep sleep every 45–60 minutes. This is not a phase that passes. It is a permanent change.

The problem: babies who have learned to fall asleep only with feeding, rocking, or a pacifier will now wake at every sleep cycle transition and need that same help to fall back asleep. The only lasting solution is sleep training — helping your baby learn to fall asleep independently.

Prepare now, not after it hits

The window before the regression arrives (right now, at 3 months) is the best time to lay groundwork: consistent bedtime routine, dark room, white noise, and putting your baby down drowsy but awake — not fully asleep. This teaches early self-settling before the regression makes everything harder.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 3-month-old be hitting?

At 3 months: consistent social smile, cooing back-and-forth, head held up during tummy time, early rolling precursors, visual tracking, recognizing familiar voices. The 4-month sleep regression is on the horizon — typically hits between 3.5 and 4.5 months.

When do babies start rolling over?

Rolling front to back typically begins between 3 and 5 months. Rolling back to front follows at 5–6 months. Tummy time is the primary driver — babies who get more tummy time roll earlier. Not rolling in either direction by 6 months is worth mentioning at the next visit.

When should I stop swaddling?

Stop swaddling the moment your baby shows any signs of attempting to roll — rocking onto one shoulder, twisting hips, getting a knee under their body. These signs typically appear 2–4 months. A swaddled baby who rolls face-down cannot push themselves back up — it's a suffocation risk. The transition is immediate, not gradual. Transition to an arms-free wearable sleep sack.

What is the 4-month sleep regression?

Around 3.5–4.5 months, your baby's sleep permanently matures to adult-like cycles (light and deep sleep every 45–60 minutes). Babies who can only fall asleep with feeding or rocking will wake at every cycle transition needing that help again. It's not a phase — it's permanent. The solution is sleep training so your baby can fall asleep independently. Start laying groundwork now with consistent routines and putting them down drowsy but awake.

Is it normal for a 3-month-old to have head wobble?

Some wobble at 3 months is completely normal. Steady head control — holding the head up for several seconds without collapsing when held upright — is expected by 4 months. If your baby cannot lift their head at all during tummy time at 3 months, mention it at the next visit.
