2 Month Milestones

2 Month Baby Milestones — The Social Smile and Your Baby's First Vaccines

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·7 min read

The social smile is the milestone that changes everything at 2 months — it's your baby's first intentional social act, and it's also the earliest reliable early screening indicator for autism. Here's what it means, the full 2-month checklist, and everything at the first vaccine appointment.

Month two is when the one-way street of newborn care starts becoming a conversation. Your baby smiles back. They coo. They follow your face with their eyes. The neurology that makes language, attachment, and social development possible is switching on — and if you know what to look for, you can see it happening in real time.

The Social Smile — The Most Important Milestone of Month Two

The social smile is a smile triggered specifically by a face and a voice. Not by gas. Not by a dream. Not by a ceiling fan. By you.

It typically first appears around 6 weeks and should be consistent by 2 months. Before this, newborns may smile reflexively — in their sleep, while gassy, in response to a loud noise. Those are involuntary movements. The social smile is different: it has a clear trigger (your face and voice), a brief delay (your baby processes what they're seeing before responding), and eye contact.

How to tell a social smile from a reflex smile

Social smile: triggered by a face and voice, involves eye contact, takes a beat to appear after you smile at them.

Reflex smile: appears while sleeping, while gassy, randomly without a social trigger. No eye contact. Comes and goes.

The social smile is also the beginning of serve-and-return — the back-and-forth emotional exchange that drives language and attachment development. Every time you smile, they smile back; every time they vocalize, you respond — those exchanges are literally building neural pathways.

⚠️ Flag — no social smile by 3 months

Absent social smiling by 3 months is one of the earliest and most reliable behavioral indicators screened for autism spectrum disorder. If your baby is not showing any social smile by 2–3 months, bring it up at the next visit or call before then. Don't wait.

Motor Milestones at 2 Months

  • Head lifting during tummy time — By 2 months, most babies can briefly lift their head 45° during tummy time. Head control is developing but not yet steady — support the head whenever you're holding your baby upright.
  • Visual tracking — Following a slowly moving object with their eyes. Hold a face or bright object about 8–12 inches away and move it slowly side to side. Your baby should track it.
  • Hands open more often — The tight newborn fist begins to relax. Your baby will start swiping at objects and briefly holding things placed in their hand.
  • Startles at sounds — A consistent startle reflex (Moro reflex) to sudden loud sounds indicates normal hearing. This will fade over the next few months.

Tummy Time — Start Now, Not Later

Tummy time builds the neck, shoulder, and back muscles your baby needs for every physical milestone that follows: rolling, sitting, crawling, standing. It also prevents positional flat head (plagiocephaly), which is almost entirely preventable in the first months and significantly harder to address after 6 months.

The AAP recommends working up to 20 minutes of tummy time per day by 4 months — not all at once, spread across the day in multiple short sessions. At 2 months, start with whatever your baby tolerates and build up. Using a parent's chest for tummy time (while you're reclined) is a valid option for babies who resist the floor.

⚠️ If tummy time hasn't been happening consistently — start now

It's not too late before 3 months. Go slowly, build up in 30-second increments, and use the chest position if the floor is resisted. The window to prevent positional flat head closes by about 6 months.

Language Milestones at 2 Months

Cooing

Cooing — soft vowel sounds like "ooh" and "ahh" — begins around 6–8 weeks and should be consistent by 2 months. It's your baby's first intentional vocal communication, and the right response is conversation: coo back, pause, let them respond. This turn-taking is the earliest form of the back-and-forth that all language is built on.

No cooing by 3 months is a language flag worth mentioning at the next visit.

Hearing

Most hospitals do a newborn hearing screen before discharge. If yours did and it passed, you're tracking. If not — or if you have any concern about your baby's responsiveness to sounds — schedule an outpatient audiology test. Early intervention for hearing loss is highly effective when started before 6 months; outcomes drop significantly after that window.

The 2-Month Well-Child Visit

The 2-month visit is the first major vaccine appointment and the first formal developmental check. Your pediatrician will assess: social smile, cooing, head control during tummy time, and visual tracking.

Your baby will likely be fussy and may run a low fever in the 24–48 hours after vaccination. This is a normal immune response. Infant acetaminophen (ask your pediatrician for the dose based on your baby's weight) can help with discomfort.

What to ask at the 2-month visit

  • Is the social smile consistent? What's the flag timeline?
  • How much tummy time should we be doing now — and what if they resist it?
  • What's coming at 4 months developmentally?
  • When should we start thinking about swaddle transition? (Rolling signs are typically 2–4 months)

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 2-month-old be hitting?

At 2 months, most babies show a social smile in response to faces and voices, coo and make vowel sounds, follow moving objects with their eyes, lift their head briefly during tummy time, and startle at sudden loud sounds. The 2-month well-child visit is the first major vaccine appointment and covers all of these.

What is the social smile and when does it appear?

The social smile is a genuine smile triggered by a face and voice — not gas or a reflex. It first appears around 6 weeks and should be consistent by 2 months. It's your baby's first intentional social act and the start of two-way emotional communication. Absent social smiling by 3 months is one of the earliest clinical red flags for autism spectrum disorder.

What vaccines does a baby get at 2 months?

The 2-month visit is the first major vaccine appointment: DTaP, Hib, PCV15, rotavirus (oral), polio, and hepatitis B (dose 2). Your baby will likely be fussy and may have a low fever for 24–48 hours afterward — this is normal. Ask your pediatrician about infant acetaminophen dosing based on your baby's weight.

When should I start tummy time?

From day one — even brief sessions on a parent's chest count. Build toward 20 minutes per day spread throughout the day by 4 months. Tummy time builds the muscles needed for rolling, sitting, crawling, and standing. It also prevents positional flat head. If your baby resists the floor, try tummy time on your chest while you're reclined.

When do babies start cooing?

Cooing — soft vowel sounds like "ooh" and "ahh" — begins around 6–8 weeks and should be established by 2 months. Respond to every coo with talking and pausing for a turn — this back-and-forth is the earliest foundation of language development.
