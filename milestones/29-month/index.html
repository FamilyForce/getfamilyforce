29 Month Milestones

29 Month Milestones — One Month From the 30-Month Checkup

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

The 30-month visit was specifically added to the AAP schedule to catch language delays that develop between 24 and 36 months. Here's what will be assessed — and how to prepare.

What the 30-Month Visit Assesses — Prepare Now

The 30-month well-child visit was added specifically to the AAP schedule for developmental surveillance. The gap from 24 to 36 months is long enough that language delays can become entrenched. This visit closes that gap.

  • Three-word sentences — Must be regular and consistent. This is the primary language checkpoint.
  • Pronouns — "I," "me," "mine" in consistent use. Persistent use of own name instead of "I" is flagged.
  • Two-step commands — Following two-step instructions without gestures.
  • Cooperative play — Playing with other children, not just alongside them.
  • Jumping in place — Both feet off the ground. If absent, motor eval may be considered.
  • Potty training — Progress assessed. Not yet trained is not automatically a concern at 30 months, but readiness and effort are discussed.
  • Behavior — Tantrum frequency, impulse control, sleep, routines.

⚠️ No three-word sentences by 29 months

This is the primary language flag at next month's visit. Speech language evaluation at 30 months has strong evidence of effectiveness — the window for early intervention is still wide open. Don't wait to see if it resolves. Flag it at the visit and ask about a referral.

Language at 29 Months

Most children at 29 months are regularly producing three-word sentences, with some producing four-word phrases. Vocabulary is typically 400–600+ words. Strangers should understand at least 50–60% of what your child says at this age (the 75% intelligibility target is at 36 months).

Color Naming — Starting to Emerge

Color naming typically begins around 24–30 months. Color names are cognitively harder than object names: they describe a property of things rather than a thing itself. Most 29-month-olds can identify 1–3 colors reliably, though the range is wide. Practice during daily life: "the red cup," "your blue shirt," "the green grass." The repetition in context is what makes color names stick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 29-month-old be hitting?

Three-word sentences regular, pronouns consistent, two-step commands followed, beginning color naming, cooperative play present, jumping established, potty training in progress, strangers understanding most of what they say.

What does the 30-month well-child visit assess?

Three-word sentences (primary flag if absent), pronoun use, two-step instruction following, cooperative play, jumping in place, potty training progress, behavior, and sleep. The visit was added specifically for developmental surveillance between the 24-month and 36-month visits.

When should toddlers know their colors?

Color naming typically begins around 24–30 months. Most children can identify 1–3 colors reliably by 30 months. Not knowing any colors at all by 36 months is worth mentioning at the 36-month visit — though color perception is usually fine even when naming is delayed.
