# 32 Months: Four-Word Sentences and Daytime Dryness

At 32 months, sentences are getting longer, daytime potty training should be established, and the 3-year checkup is four months out. Language and self-care are both accelerating.

---

Month 32 is characterized by a sense of competence. Your child can tell you what they need, negotiate (badly but sincerely), explain what happened, and manage many self-care tasks with minimal help. The enormous developmental work of the first three years is becoming visible in daily life.

Potty training is the practical focus for many families at 32 months. Daytime dryness should be largely established by now. Nighttime dryness comes later — often not until 4–5 years — and is hormonally controlled, not a training achievement.

## Language Milestones at 32 Months

- Four-word sentences — regular ("I want the big red truck." "Daddy is at work today." "Can I have some more?")
- Asks "why" constantly — the why phase peaks around 30–36 months; answer every why with a short causal explanation
- Retells simple events — "We painted and then we had snack." Sequence is still rough. That's fine.
- Counts objects to 3–5 — one-to-one correspondence for small numbers; touch each one while counting
- Names 4+ colors — red, blue, yellow, green at minimum; orange, purple, black, white often following

## Potty Training: Where Things Should Stand

With First Son, I started potty training when someone told me he seemed ready. He was 25 months and showing some interest. What I didn't know was that I was reading interest as readiness, and those aren't the same thing. He could sit on the potty but couldn't reliably signal when he needed to go. We spent three months in a cycle of accidents, frustration on my part, and regression on his. By 32 months he was trained, but the process was harder than it needed to be.

With Second Son, I knew the actual readiness markers before I started — not interest, but readiness: can he stay dry for 90 minutes, does he know when he's going, can he pull pants up and down on his own, does he have the language to communicate urgency. He hit all of them around 28 months. Training took 10 days. Not because he was easier, but because the window was right and I wasn't fighting his developmental timeline.

Nighttime dryness is separate. First Son wet the bed until almost age 5, and I made the mistake of treating it like a training failure. It's not. The hormone vasopressin activates when it activates. With Second Son I accepted nighttime pull-ups as the practical solution they are. That alone removed a significant source of stress from bedtime.

By 32 months, daytime potty training should be largely established for children who started training at readiness. Occasional accidents — especially when distracted, excited, or not near a bathroom — continue to 36+ months. That's normal.

**Bedwetting until age 5 is within clinical norms.** The hormone vasopressin, which reduces urine production at night, doesn't fully activate in many children until 4–5 years. Nighttime accidents are not a training failure and should not be treated as such. Pull-ups at night while daytime trained is a valid, effective strategy.

If daytime training isn't established at 32 months: assess whether readiness was truly present when you started. If regression has happened (was trained, now having accidents): check for stressors (new sibling, change in routine, transition to a new setting). Regression is usually temporary and resolves with consistency and patience.

## Social Development at 32 Months

- Parallel and cooperative play both present — cooperative play is increasing in duration and complexity
- Beginning to understand rules and fairness — "That's not fair" signals fairness is being processed; acknowledge it and explain the actual rule
- Specific friend preferences — names specific children they like; friendships are real and meaningful at 32 months
- Separates more easily — drop-offs are usually easier than at 18–24 months

## Motor at 32 Months

- Pedaling a tricycle — circular pedaling motion is established or establishing; steering takes longer
- Alternating feet on stairs — many 32-month-olds beginning to alternate feet going down as well as up
- Draws a circle and vertical line — not perfect; controlled mark-making is the milestone

## What to Do Right Now

1. **Ask "what happened today?" every day.** Even if the answer is vague or sequence-confused, you're practicing narrative construction. It's a skill that predicts reading comprehension at school age.
2. **Normalize nighttime accidents.** If your child is daytime trained but wetting at night, respond calmly. Change the sheets, move on. Shaming or punishing nighttime accidents is counterproductive and unnecessary.
3. **Begin counting past 5 objects.** At meals, at bath time, on walks. "How many apples are there? Let's count." The window for counting objects 1–5 is active; pushing toward 10 is the next target.

The 3-year checkup is four months away. Language is the primary thing that visit will assess. Continue the daily work.
