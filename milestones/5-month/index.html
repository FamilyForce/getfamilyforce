5 Month Milestones

5 Month Baby Milestones — Rolling, Tummy Time Goals, and Sleep Changes

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·7 min read

Five months is when rolling becomes a primary mode of locomotion and tummy time hits its stride. The sleep regression may still be lingering. Here's what's happening and what to do about all of it.

Month five is the bridge between the newborn phase and the mobile phase. Your baby is now rolling intentionally, reaching for and grasping objects, laughing, and starting to show clear preferences. The world they can access is rapidly expanding — and physically, they're building the core strength that will make sitting, crawling, and walking possible.

Motor Milestones at 5 Months

  • Rolling tummy to back — established — Should be consistent by now. If not, increase tummy time immediately.
  • Rolling back to tummy — emerging — Requires more core strength. Typically follows front-to-back by a few weeks. Some babies roll both ways almost simultaneously.
  • Extended arm push-up during tummy time — Lifting the whole chest off the floor on straight arms. This is the precursor to sitting up and eventual crawling.
  • Intentional reaching and grasping — Deliberately reaching toward objects and grasping with the whole hand. Transferring objects hand-to-hand is beginning.
  • Bearing weight on legs — When held in standing position, your baby pushes down and bears significant weight. This is exploratory — not a sign they're ready to walk, but building necessary strength.

⚠️ Not rolling front to back by 5 months

Mention it at your next pediatrician visit. It's usually a sign they need more tummy time practice — but it's worth flagging. Not rolling in either direction by 6 months is a more significant flag.

Tummy Time — Hit the 20-Minute Target

The AAP recommends 20 minutes of tummy time per day by 4 months, spread across multiple sessions. At 5 months, you should be at or close to this. If you're not, start building now — the motor development window for flat head prevention is closing around 6 months.

Tummy time at 5 months looks very different from the newborn phase. Your baby is now pushing up on extended arms, looking around freely, tracking objects by turning their head, and possibly pivoting in circles or rocking forward and backward. Keep doing it even though rolling is established — it's directly building the core strength for sitting and crawling.

Swaddle — should be gone by now

If your baby is rolling and still swaddled, stop tonight. A rolling baby who is swaddled cannot push themselves up or turn their face away from the mattress. This is a suffocation risk. Transition to an arms-free wearable sleep sack immediately.

Language and Social Milestones at 5 Months

Laughing and squealing

Real laughter — triggered by peek-a-boo, funny sounds, being lifted and swooped — is a key social milestone at 5 months. It signals the serve-and-return social exchange is deepening. Play that gets a laugh is worth repeating.

Babbling — should be starting

Consonant-vowel babbling ("ba," "da," "ma") typically begins around 4–5 months and should be emerging now if it hasn't. This is the bridge from cooing to words. Babbling frequency and variety at 5–6 months predicts vocabulary size at 18 months. Respond to every babble — the conversation you're having now matters.

Sound imitation

Around 4–5 months, babies begin to imitate sounds. Stick out your tongue — they may copy it. Make a sound — they may try to match the pitch. This is one of the earliest signs of social learning, the capacity to observe, process, and replicate behavior that all language and development is built on.

Sleep at 5 Months — The Regression Aftermath

If the 4-month sleep regression hit hard, you may still be in the thick of it at 5 months. The regression itself typically peaks between 3.5 and 4.5 months, but for babies who haven't developed independent sleep skills, frequent night waking can persist indefinitely.

The distinction: if your baby was sleeping reasonably well before 3.5 months and then stopped, that's the regression. If they've always woken frequently, it's a sleep association issue. Both have the same solution: helping your baby learn to fall asleep independently. At 5 months, most sleep training methods are age-appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 5-month-old be hitting?

Rolling tummy to back (established), beginning to roll back to tummy, extended arm push-up during tummy time, reaching and grasping intentionally, laughing, babbling beginning, bearing weight on legs when held standing.

When do babies roll from back to front?

Rolling back to front typically follows front-to-back by a few weeks, arriving around 5–6 months. Not rolling in either direction by 6 months is worth mentioning to your pediatrician.

How much tummy time should a 5-month-old have?

At or near 20 minutes per day, spread across multiple sessions. The AAP target of 20 minutes/day is for 4 months — by 5 months you should be meeting or exceeding it. Continue tummy time even after rolling is established; it builds sitting and crawling strength.

Is the 4-month sleep regression still going at 5 months?

For some babies, yes. The regression can persist 2–6 weeks. If your baby is still waking frequently, the new sleep architecture is permanent — the path through is sleep training so they can fall asleep independently. Most methods are appropriate from 4–5 months onward.
