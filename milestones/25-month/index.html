25 Month Milestones

25 Month Milestones — Three-Word Sentences Are Starting

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

After the vocabulary spurt comes grammar. Three-word sentences are starting to emerge, sentences are getting longer, and your child is starting to tell you things rather than just label them.

Three-Word Sentences — From Labels to Grammar

Three-word combinations — "I want milk," "daddy come here," "no more nap" — mark the transition from telegraphic speech to early grammar. They typically begin emerging between 24 and 30 months and represent a qualitative shift: your child is now constructing sentences with structure, not just stringing nouns together.

Three-word sentences usually follow naturally from the two-word combination milestone. If two-word phrases are well established, three-word phrases are likely forming. The model-and-expand technique still works: if your child says "want cookie," respond with "you want a cookie?" You're providing the expanded model without correcting them.

⚠️ Still primarily using two-word phrases at 25 months

This is worth tracking. Three-word sentences are assessed at the 30-month visit. If two-word phrases are present but three-word sentences haven't appeared by 27–28 months, flag it at the 30-month visit. Early speech evaluation at this age is highly effective.

Motor Milestones at 25 Months

  • Jumping in place — Both feet off the ground. This is still developing for many toddlers at 25 months; it's fully expected by 30 months.
  • Running with direction changes — Smooth, deliberate, changing direction without falling.
  • Undressing independently — Pulling off socks, shoes (especially velcro), and loose pants. Undressing always precedes dressing — it requires less precision. This skill matters for potty training.
  • Drawing — lines and beginning circles — Intentional marks on paper. Closed circles are emerging; recognizable shapes are months away.

Potty Training — Reading Readiness

Some children show potty training readiness signs between 18–24 months. At 25 months, if the readiness signs are there, training is appropriate to start. If they're not, starting anyway leads to a longer and more conflict-heavy process. The research is consistent: waiting for readiness signs — not starting at a fixed age — predicts a faster, lower-conflict experience.

Readiness signs to look for: staying dry for 2+ hours, signaling a need before going (not after), showing interest in the toilet or underwear, being able to follow simple two-step instructions, and being able to pull pants up and down.

Keep a floor potty visible and pressure-free. Let them sit on it fully clothed. Familiarity before training starts reduces fear significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 25-month-old be hitting?

Three-word sentences beginning, two-word combinations well established, 200+ words, running and jumping in place, undressing independently, beginning to draw intentional shapes, potty training readiness signs possibly present.

When do 3-word sentences start?

Three-word sentences typically emerge between 24 and 30 months. They follow naturally from two-word combinations. If two-word phrases are well established but three-word sentences haven't appeared by 27–28 months, flag it at the 30-month well-child visit.

When should I start potty training?

When the readiness signs are present — not at a fixed age. Signs include: staying dry 2+ hours, signaling before going, showing interest in the toilet, following two-step instructions, and being able to pull pants up and down. Starting before these signs are present leads to longer, harder training.
