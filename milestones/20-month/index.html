20 Month Milestones

20 Month Milestones — Vocabulary Is Accelerating and Independence Is Growing

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Twenty months is deep in the vocabulary spurt — new words are arriving fast. It's also when independence drive peaks, which means more conflict but also more learning.

Language — Vocabulary Spurt in Full Effect

Most toddlers at 20 months have 30–50+ words and are adding new ones rapidly — sometimes daily. The vocabulary spurt that began after the 10-word milestone is now in full swing. The target is 50 words by 24 months, which is the primary language checkpoint at the two-year visit.

Two-word combinations should be emerging or established. If your toddler has 30–40 words but no two-word combinations yet, they're likely close — the two-word milestone typically follows the vocabulary base rather than arriving on a schedule.

Keep using expansion during conversations. When your child says "dog," expand: "the brown dog," "the dog is running," "big dog outside." Every expansion gives your toddler a model of richer language to work toward.

Motor Milestones at 20 Months

  • Running confidently — Changing direction without falling. If your 20-month-old walks well but shows no attempt to run, mention it at the next visit as part of gross motor review.
  • Climbing up and down — Climbing furniture confidently. Going up stairs with support. Going down stairs starting to develop (backing down or sitting).
  • Kicking a stationary ball — If your 20-month-old cannot kick a stationary ball at all, mention it at the next gross motor review.
  • Holding a spoon — Self-feeding with a spoon with increasing accuracy. Expect mess — it's part of learning.

The Independence Drive

At 20 months, "I do it" is the operating principle. Your toddler wants to pour their own water, put on their own shoes, choose their own food. This is healthy and developmentally important — autonomy and competence are building. The conflict it creates is the side effect.

Offer choices within limits: "do you want the red cup or the blue cup?" gives the sense of control without creating an open-ended battle. Prepare extra time for tasks they insist on doing themselves. The investment pays off in a more cooperative child by 24–30 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 20-month-old be hitting?

Running confidently, climbing, kicking a ball, 30–50+ words, two-word combinations emerging, strong drive for independence, parallel play, scribbling, self-feeding with a spoon.

How many words should a 20-month-old have?

Typically 30–50 words, with many toddlers in the vocabulary spurt having more. The target is 50 words by 24 months. Two-word combinations should be emerging or present.

Is it normal for a 20-month-old to be defiant?

Yes — the independence drive at 20 months is developmentally normal and healthy. Offering choices within limits, preparing extra time for self-directed tasks, and staying calm during protests are the most effective approaches. The drive for autonomy at this age predicts healthy independence later.
