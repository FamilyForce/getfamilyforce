17 Month Milestones

17 Month Milestones — Pretend Play Is One of the Most Important Windows Right Now

By Jack Hartley ·Updated March 31, 2026 ·6 min read

Most parents think of pretend play as just play. The research says it's one of the strongest predictors of language ability, emotional regulation, and creativity at school age. Here's what's happening at 17 months and why it matters.

Pretend Play — Why This Window Is Critical

Early pretend play — one simple action, one object — typically begins 12–17 months. By 17 months, it should be well established: feeding a stuffed animal, talking into a toy phone, pretending to sleep or cook or drive.

What makes pretend play developmentally significant: it requires symbolic thinking — the ability to hold a mental representation separate from reality. When your toddler holds a banana to their ear as a phone, they're demonstrating that objects can stand in for other things. This is the same cognitive architecture that language uses.

Research by Jerome Singer and Sandra Russ links rich pretend play at 2–3 years to better emotional regulation and creativity at school age. Children who engage in more pretend play have larger vocabularies, better narrative ability, and stronger theory of mind (the ability to understand that other people have different perspectives).

What to do

Get on the floor and model pretend play. Pretend to feed a stuffed animal. Set up a pretend kitchen. Act out simple scenes. Your toddler will watch, imitate, and then initiate. The richness of early pretend play is directly shaped by how much adults participate in it. Screens don't generate pretend play. Unstructured play with objects and a responsive adult does.

⚠️ No pretend play at all by 18 months

This is one of the indicators reviewed in the M-CHAT autism screening at the 18-month visit. If your toddler shows no pretend play and no symbolic use of objects by 18 months, raise it specifically. Don't wait for the screening form to prompt it.

Motor Milestones at 17 Months

  • Running — established — Still stiff-legged, but deliberate and faster than walking. Falls when changing direction quickly are normal.
  • Stair climbing with support — Up stairs with a hand or railing, one step at a time. Going down is still harder — most toddlers back down or sit and scoot.
  • Stacking 3–4 blocks — Fine motor is developing. Frustration when towers fall is normal (and a good emotion regulation practice moment).
  • Beginning to kick a ball — Kicking a stationary ball. Accuracy is poor but the attempt is the milestone.

Language at 17 Months

Most toddlers have 10–20 words at 17 months. The vocabulary spurt — rapid daily vocabulary growth — is often in full swing. New words may appear every day. Keep expanding: when your child says "dog," respond with "big dog" or "dog running." Expansion accelerates vocabulary faster than drilling new words.

Two-word combinations are still months away for most toddlers — they typically arrive after the 50-word milestone, usually at 18–24 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 17-month-old be hitting?

Walking confidently, running (stiff-legged), stair climbing with support, stacking 3–4 blocks, 10–20 words, well-established pretend play, beginning to kick a ball, parallel play alongside other children.

When does pretend play start?

Early pretend play begins around 12–17 months and should be well established by 17–18 months. Complex multi-step pretend play develops from about 20–24 months. No pretend play at all by 18 months is worth raising at the M-CHAT visit.

How many words should a 17-month-old have?

Typically 10–20 words at 17 months, with many toddlers in the midst of the vocabulary spurt. The target is 10+ words by 15–18 months. Fewer than 10 words at 18 months is a flag for the M-CHAT well-child visit.
