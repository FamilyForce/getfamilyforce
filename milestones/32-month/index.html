32 Month Milestones

32 Month Milestones — Four-Word Sentences and the Road to Full Fluency

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Language is becoming genuinely expressive at 32 months. Four-word sentences are standard, strangers can understand most of what your child says, and narrative — telling you about things that happened — is starting.

Language — Four-Word Sentences and Intelligibility

By 32 months, most children are regularly producing sentences of 4 or more words. The progression from two-word combinations (20 months) to three-word sentences (28 months) to four-word phrases (32 months) is fast — this is the most explosive period of language development in a human life.

Intelligibility — how much of what your child says is understood by unfamiliar adults — should now be roughly 50–65%. The 36-month target is 75%. If strangers consistently cannot understand more than half of what your 32-month-old says, mention it at the 36-month visit.

Narrative — Telling You What Happened

Around 30–33 months, children begin to construct simple narratives: telling you about something that happened, recounting an event from earlier in the day. "We went park. Dog was big. I ran." This is more than language — it's memory organization and temporal understanding. It's also the skill that school-age reading comprehension is built on.

Encourage narrative by asking open-ended questions about past events: "What happened at the park today?" Listen fully. Don't complete their sentences. The cognitive work of constructing the narrative happens in the pause.

Color Naming — Should Be Established

By 32 months, most children reliably identify 2–4 colors. Color names are abstract (properties of things, not things themselves) and take longer to solidify than object names. Continue reinforcing during daily life: "grab your blue jacket," "the red one, please," "look at the yellow flower." Context makes it stick faster than drills.

Fine Motor at 32 Months

  • Drawing recognizable shapes — Circles and approximate squares. Figures (faces) start to appear around 30–36 months.
  • Turning doorknobs — A moment of great freedom and great hazard simultaneously. Review childproofing accordingly.
  • Stringing large beads — Requires eye-hand coordination and bilateral coordination.
  • Using a fork consistently — Stabbing and self-feeding with a fork. Not spearing perfectly, but consistently attempting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 32-month-old be hitting?

4-word sentences, strangers understanding 50–65% of speech, narrative ability starting (recounting events), 2–4 colors named reliably, drawing recognizable shapes, fork use, potty training complete or near-complete.

When should strangers understand what a toddler says?

By 36 months, the target is 75% intelligibility to unfamiliar adults. At 32 months, 50–65% is typical. If strangers consistently understand less than half of what your child says at 32+ months, flag it at the 36-month visit for speech assessment.

How do I build my toddler's narrative skills?

Ask open-ended questions about past events and listen fully without completing their sentences. 'What happened at the park?' 'Who did you see?' The work of constructing the narrative happens in the pause. Don't rush it. Narrative skill is the foundation of school-age reading comprehension.
