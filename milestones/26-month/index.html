26 Month Milestones — Cooperative Play Is Beginning

By Jack Hartley·Updated March 31, 2026·6 min read

Around 26 months, the first real cooperative play begins. Not just playing side by side, but actually playing together. Here's what that looks like and what it means.

Cooperative Play — Playing With, Not Just Next To

For the first two years, toddlers engage primarily in parallel play — each child doing their own thing in proximity to others. Around 24–27 months, cooperative play begins: shared games, negotiated rules, taking turns, a common goal. It's fragile and brief at first, but it's real.

Cooperative play requires theory of mind — understanding that other people have different intentions and perspectives. It also requires impulse control and the ability to wait. These are all still developing. When cooperative play breaks down into conflict at 26 months, that's expected. The play broke down because the skills are new, not because anything is wrong.

How to support it

Simple structured games work better than unstructured play at this age — a ball you roll back and forth, a simple chase game, stacking blocks together and knocking them down. The structure compensates for the still-developing turn-taking and impulse control. Don't force sharing — model it. "Daddy is sharing his cup. Now your turn."

Emotion Labeling — Name It to Tame It

Children who have words for their emotions regulate them significantly better. The research is consistent: labeling an emotion activates the prefrontal cortex and reduces the amygdala's stress response. In plain terms — naming the feeling reduces its intensity. This starts with the parent doing the labeling: "You look frustrated that the block fell down." "I can see you're really excited about the park."

Use simple, clear words: happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, proud. Name your own emotions too. You are building their emotional vocabulary — even if they never repeat the words back to you in the moment, the wiring is happening.

Language at 26 Months

Three-word sentences should be appearing regularly. Vocabulary is typically 200–300+ words. Pronouns ("I," "me," "mine") should be consistently in use — if they're still substituting their own name for "I," flag it at the 30-month visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What milestones should a 26-month-old be hitting?

Three-word sentences regular, 200–300+ words, pronouns in use, beginning cooperative play, jumping in place, undressing independently, potty training in progress or readiness signs present.

When does cooperative play start?

Cooperative play — playing together with shared rules or goals, not just parallel — begins emerging around 24–27 months. It's brief and fragile at first. Conflicts when cooperative play breaks down are normal. Full cooperative play develops through age 3–4.

How do I teach my 2-year-old to share?

Model it rather than forcing it. Research on sharing shows that mandating sharing ('give it to your sister now') is less effective than modeling — doing it yourself and narrating it. Turn-taking games with simple structure help because they build the impulse control that sharing requires.
