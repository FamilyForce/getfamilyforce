# 29 Months: One Month Out From the 30-Month Visit

The 30-month well-child visit is four weeks away. The AAP added this visit specifically for developmental surveillance between the 2-year and 3-year checkups. Month 29 is the preparation window.

---

The 30-month visit was added to the AAP Periodicity Schedule in 2016 specifically because the 6-month gap between the 2-year and 3-year checkups was identified as too long for developmental surveillance. Language, social, and motor development are all moving fast in this period, and missing concerns until age 3 means missing the window for the most effective early intervention.

At 29 months, you have four weeks. Use them to observe clearly and prepare for a useful visit.

With First Son, I walked into the 30-month visit without any observations prepared. I didn't know the visit had been added specifically to catch developmental concerns, and I didn't know what it was looking for. The pediatrician asked questions I couldn't answer: "Is he putting together 3-word sentences regularly? Can strangers understand him?" I said "I think so" to most of them. The visit was useful but far less useful than it could have been.

With Second Son, I spent the week before writing down what I actually observed. Three-word sentences I'd heard that week. Colors he could name. Whether he followed multi-step instructions. Specific things, not impressions. That changed the visit entirely. The pediatrician had real data to work with. One thing I'd noted — a slight drop in clarity to strangers after a growth spurt — led to a conversation that wouldn't have happened if I'd come in empty-handed.

Four weeks is enough time to build a useful picture. Watch specifically. Write down what you see. The visit is designed to find what casual observation misses.

## Language at 29 Months

- Three-word sentences — routine; if still primarily at two words, bring it up at the visit
- Vocabulary 200–400 words — wide range is normal; direction of travel matters as much as total count
- 75% understandable to strangers — the 30-month target; note if family understands but strangers regularly can't
- Names colors reliably — 3–4 colors by 30 months is the target
- Follows multi-step instructions — "Go get your shoes, then come back here"

### What the 30-month visit will assess

Language: three-word sentences, speech clarity, vocabulary. Motor: running, jumping, stair climbing. Social: cooperative play, empathy signs, sharing attempts. Behavior: screen time, sleep, eating. The visit uses a validated screening tool — often the ASQ-3 (Ages and Stages Questionnaire) — to assess developmental status across all domains. Coming prepared with examples and observations makes the visit significantly more useful.

## Social Development at 29 Months

- Cooperative play — more sustained; 5–10 minutes of shared play before breakdown; simple role-based games; turn-taking
- Empathy becoming consistent — reacts when others are hurt or upset; attempts to comfort; names others' emotions sometimes
- Sharing beginning — voluntary sharing starting to appear with familiar peers; don't force it, notice and label it
- Tantrums — much reduced from the 18–21 month peak; less frequent and more manageable as language fills in

## Motor at 29 Months

- Running confidently on varied terrain — slightly uneven surfaces; better balance on inclines
- Jumping from a low step — two-foot landing with reasonable balance; height of jump increasing
- Drawing simple shapes — vertical lines, horizontal lines, round scribbles approaching circles
- Dressing with minimal help — puts on shoes (without tying), pulls up pants, manages some buttons

## What to Do Right Now

1. **Write down specific language examples.** Three-word sentences you've heard this week. Colors named. Two-step instructions followed. Real, specific examples for the 30-month visit are more useful than general impressions.
2. **Note any concerns.** Behavior you've been wondering about. Language that seems to have plateaued. Social patterns that seem different from peers. Bring them to the visit — this is your designated opportunity to raise them without waiting until age 3.
3. **Confirm the visit is booked.** If you haven't scheduled the 30-month visit yet, do it today. Getting a same-day appointment at a busy pediatric practice can take weeks.

The 30-month visit is designed to catch what parents and pediatricians both miss between the 2-year and 3-year checkups. Go in prepared and you'll get the most out of it.
