## 👁️ 2–3 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 4 | Recognises parents' faces | By 2 months baby prefers your face over strangers'. Visual recognition — distinct from the social smile. | Feldman/Stanford |
| 5 | Colour discrimination develops | By 3–4 months infants can distinguish red/green shades and begin showing colour preferences. | CDC 2022 |

## 🤲 4–6 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 6 | Bears weight on legs when held standing | Half of babies do this at 4 months. Muscle development and nerve maturation, not early walking. | WhatToExpect |
| 7 | Social referencing begins | At 5–6 months baby starts looking at your face to decide if something is safe or scary. First sign of emotional intelligence. | StatPearls |
| 8 | Intentional reaching with both hands | Grasping shifts from reflexive to deliberate around 4–5 months. Key cognitive milestone, not just motor. | CDC 2022 |

## 🛡️ 6–9 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 9 | Separation anxiety begins (8–10 months) | Different from stranger anxiety. This is distress when caregiver leaves. Normal and healthy. | AAP / WhatToExpect |
| 10 | The 9-month sleep regression | Often the most disruptive regression. Caused by the crawling/standing developmental leap. Parents are blindsided. | WhatToExpect |
| 11 | Waves bye-bye (9–10 months) | First communicative gesture. A key CDC 2022 benchmark at the 9-month visit. | CDC 2022 |
| 12 | Drinks from a straw | Developmentally appropriate at 9–12 months. Better oral muscle development than sippy cups. | AAP |
| 13 | Claps hands (8–10 months) | CDC 2022 benchmark at the 9-month visit. Key early imitation skill. | CDC 2022 |

## 🚶 9–12 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 14 | Cruising — walks along furniture (9–12 months) | The bridge between pulling to stand and independent walking. Parents often miss it as a milestone. | CDC 2022 |
| 15 | Plays peek-a-boo intentionally | By 10 months baby initiates peek-a-boo. Shows object permanence AND social turn-taking in one milestone. | StatPearls |
| 16 | Points to share interest (proto-declarative pointing) | Different from pointing to demand. "Look at that!" = wanting to share an experience. A significant social-cognitive leap. | CDC 2022 / Zubler 2022 |
| 17 | 📅 15-month well-child visit | AAP ADDED this visit in 2022. Many parents don't know it exists. New developmental screening added here. | AAP 2022 |

## 🏃 12–18 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 18 | Walking backward (~15 months) | Appears a few months after forward walking. Signals improved balance and spatial awareness. | WhatToExpect |
| 19 | Pretend play begins — feeds doll, puts teddy to sleep | Earliest form of imaginative play. Huge cognitive milestone. Appears earlier than most parents expect. | StatPearls |
| 20 | Points to body parts when named (15–18 months) | "Where's your nose?" A CDC 2022 benchmark at the 15-month visit. | CDC 2022 |
| 21 | Vocabulary: 5+ words at 12 months, 10 by 15 months | CDC 2022 revised these numbers upward. Existing list has 30-month target but not 12 or 15-month benchmarks. | CDC / Zubler 2022 |
| 22 | The 18-month sleep regression | Tied to language explosion and increased self-awareness. Often misdiagnosed as sleep training failure. | WhatToExpect |
| 23 | Self-recognition in a mirror (16–24 months) | Rouge test milestone. Shows emergence of self-concept. | Developmental research |

## 🗣️ 18–24 Months

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 24 | Two-word combinations (18–24 months) | "More milk," "daddy go." Existing list has the 30-month 50-word target but not when two-word combos START — key earlier red flag window. | CDC 2022 |
| 25 | Sorts objects by shape and colour | 18–24 months. Early logic and categorisation. Signals readiness for shape sorters and basic puzzles. | WhatToExpect |
| 26 | Walks up stairs one foot at a time (holding rail) | ~18 months. Earlier, safer version of stair climbing (alternating feet is already in list). | WhatToExpect |
| 27 | Climbs down from furniture safely | 18–24 months. Parents baby-proof for climbing up but not down. A separate milestone with injury implications. | AAP |

## 🧠 2–3 Years

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 28 | Symbolic play — uses one object to represent another | Age 2–3. Using a banana as a phone. First abstract thinking. Qualitative leap from pretend play. | StatPearls |
| 29 | Draws a person: head with limbs (age 3) | "Tadpole drawings." First representational art. Correlates with cognitive development level. | WhatToExpect / AAP |
| 30 | Tells their full name when asked (age 2–3) | CDC benchmark. Different from recognising their name (already in list). | CDC 2022 |
| 31 | Anemia screening (9–24 months) | AAP recommends iron deficiency screening for breastfed babies at 12 months and high-risk toddlers at 24 months. Often missed. | AAP |
| 32 | The 2-year sleep regression | Coincides with toddler bed transition, potty training stress, and language explosion. The fourth regression parents encounter. | WhatToExpect |

## 🧒 3–4 Years

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 33 | Theory of mind: understanding others have different thoughts (age 3–4) | The famous "false belief task." When a child understands another person doesn't know what they know. Milestone in empathy and social cognition. | Developmental research |
| 34 | Phonemic awareness: rhyming and word play (age 3–4) | Strongest predictor of early reading success. Precedes learning to read by 1–2 years. | Feldman/Stanford 2019 |
| 35 | Cooperative play: playing WITH (not just next to) peers | Age 3–4. Parallel play transitions to cooperative play with shared rules and a shared goal. | StatPearls |
| 36 | Gender identity awareness (age 2.5–3) | Children identify their own gender and begin noticing it in others. Backed by AAP developmental guidelines. | AAP |
| 37 | Knows home address (age 4–5) | Emergency safety milestone. A child who can state their address has materially better outcomes if ever separated from a caregiver. | AAP safety guidelines |

## 🎒 4–5 Years

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 38 | Road safety basics: stop at kerb, look both ways (age 4–5) | Pedestrian death is a leading cause of child fatality. Teach the routine before school starts. | AAP injury prevention |
| 39 | Simple board games: turn-taking and losing gracefully (age 3–5) | First structured play with rules. Delayed gratification + emotional regulation in one activity. | StatPearls |
| 40 | Attention span: sustained focus for 5–10 minutes on a chosen task (age 4–5) | Kindergarten readiness predictor. Screen time at this age directly shortens this window. | McArthur 2022 (Pediatric Research) |
| 41 | Understands night vs. day, yesterday vs. tomorrow (age 4–5) | Time concept precedes clock reading. Related to but distinct from "Yesterday, today, tomorrow" (already in list). | WhatToExpect |

## 📚 5–7 Years

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 42 | Reading chapter books independently (age 7–8) | Distinct shift from decoding words to sustained narrative reading. Strong predictor of academic trajectory. | AAP |
| 43 | Telling time on a clock (age 6–7) | Combines number sense, spatial understanding, and sequence concepts. Schools expect this at year 2 entry. | WhatToExpect |
| 44 | Understanding coins and basic money (age 5–7) | Value of coins, making change. First abstract value concept. Precursor to saving/allowance milestones. | WhatToExpect |
| 45 | Washing hair independently (age 6–7) | Self-care independence milestone often overlooked. Signals readiness for more bathroom autonomy. | WhatToExpect |

## 🧩 7–10 Years

| # | Title | Notes | Source |
|---|-------|--------|--------|
| 46 | Understanding sarcasm and non-literal language (age 7–9) | "Oh great, another Monday." Grasping sarcasm, irony, and idioms marks a significant leap in social cognition. | Developmental research |
| 47 | Managing a simple allowance (age 6–8) | Earning, saving, and spending decisions. Research links early allowance to better money habits in adulthood. | WhatToExpect / AAP |
| 48 | Assigned household chores (age 6–8) | Research shows chores at this age (not later) are the strongest predictor of adult work ethic and sense of responsibility. | WhatToExpect |
