Arty is 1 months old. This is their Scout digest for the month — a quick look at what's worth your attention right now, written to take about 5 minutes to read.

Heads up: 3 windows close this month — meaning the natural developmental timing is ending. These are worth doing first.

This month's priority

Responds to sounds / startlesCloses in 4w

From birth, a baby should startle to sudden loud sounds and calm to a familiar voice. Absent or inconsistent response can be an early indicator of hearing loss.

The move

* Confirm the newborn hearing screen was completed at the hospital

Breastfeeding establishment — the first 8 weeksCloses in 4w

The first 8 weeks are the critical window for supply and demand. Your body is calibrating how much milk to make based on how often the baby removes it.

The move

* Nurse on demand, usually 8–12 times per 24-hour period

Visual stimulation — high contrast patterns, facesCloses in 4w

Newborns can only focus clearly at about 8–12 inches. High contrast patterns activate the visual cortex more powerfully than pastel colors at this stage.

The move

* Hold your face close (8–12 inches) during alert time and make slow, exaggerated expressions

Also worth knowing this month

Parent mental health — screen for postpartum depression

Postpartum depression is the most common complication of childbirth. Untreated, it affects attachment, language development, and emotional regulation in children.

The move

* Ask for the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) at the 6-week visit

Social smile appears — first intentional social act

The social smile typically appears around 6 weeks. Absent social smiling by 3 months is a clinical red flag and an early screening indicator for autism spectrum disorder.

The move

* Get close — babies can only focus clearly at 8–12 inches

Get ready for next month

2-month well-child visit

Prep: Book your 2-month well-child visit now if you haven't. Vaccines are given at this visit — have infant acetaminophen on hand for a fussier evening afterward.

Laughs out loud

Prep: Your baby's first laugh is close. Gentle tickles, funny faces, and silly sounds are the most reliable triggers. Be ready — it will make your whole week.

Rolls tummy to back

Prep: Give Arty maximum floor time on a firm surface. Rolling requires freedom to try — a bouncy seat doesn't build the same strength.

Stop swaddling when rolling signs appear

Prep: As soon as you see rolling attempts, stop swaddling immediately — arms must be free. Watch closely and be ready to transition fast.
