This month's priority

⏱ Closing this month

The 1-month well child visit

Most parents don't know this visit exists. Book it before you leave the hospital — ask for the 4-week appointment on the way out.

The 1-month visit is the first well child check after the newborn visit. It's on the AAP schedule for good reason — your baby should be back to birth weight by now, and your pediatrician will check that, plus screen you for postpartum depression at the same time.

The move

›

Schedule it at 3 to 5 weeks. If it hasn't been booked — call today.

›

Bring your feeding questions: how often, how long, how much weight gained since discharge.

›

Watch for before the visit: baby not back to birth weight by week 2–3, feeding sessions over 45 minutes, or any yellowing of skin or eyes past 2 weeks — flag all of these.

💡 Did you know

Tummy time is one of the strongest predictors of motor milestones through age 2. Babies who do consistent daily tummy time crawl earlier, stand earlier, and walk earlier. You don't need equipment or a schedule — a few minutes on your chest counts.

Also this month

⏳ Open window

Tummy time: build to 15–30 min/day by week 7

This is the single highest-impact physical habit you can start right now.

The AAP recommends 15–30 minutes of daily tummy time by 7 weeks — spread across the day, not all at once. Most babies who build this consistently roll between 3 and 5 months. The ones who skip it often struggle with rolling, crawling, and core strength later.

The move

›

Build gradually: 4–5 short sessions per day is as good as one long one.

›

Start on your chest if she hates it on the floor — chest tummy time counts.

›

A rolled towel under the chest helps. Mirrors, siblings, and your face are the best motivation.

⏱ Closing this month

Screen for postpartum depression — both of you

This one's for you, not for Olivia.

Postpartum depression affects 1 in 5 mothers and 1 in 10 fathers. It's one of the most common and most undertreated complications of childbirth. Your pediatrician should screen you at this 1-month visit — if they don't ask, you ask.

The move

›

At the 1-month visit, say: "Can we do the postpartum depression screen today?" if they don't offer it.

›

Answer honestly. The Edinburgh scale isn't a test you pass or fail — it's information your doctor uses to help you.

›

Partners: paternal postpartum depression is real, underdiagnosed, and treatable. Ask to be screened too.
