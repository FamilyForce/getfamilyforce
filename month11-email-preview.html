This month’s priority

Start the bottle-to-cup transitionCloses in 4w

After 12 months, babies become more attached to the bottle and the switch gets significantly harder. Most pediatricians recommend making the move around the first birthday. Starting at 11 months gives Emma a few weeks to adjust before the window closes.

The move

Introduce a soft-spout sippy cup at one meal a day. Put water or breast milk in it — not formula. Let her play with it at first. The goal this month is familiarity, not full replacement.

Also worth knowing this month

First words and back-and-forth babbling

Emma is building vocabulary right now even if she’s not talking yet. Around 11–12 months, most babies say their first intentional word — usually something like “mama,” “dada,” or the name of a pet. The research is clear: the more words she hears in genuine conversation, the faster this develops.

The move

Narrate your day out loud. 'I'm cutting an apple. It's cold. It's red.' Pause and wait for her to respond — even babbles count. Avoid talking at her; talk with her.

Pincer grasp and finger foods

The pincer grasp — using just the thumb and index finger — is developing fast right now. Finger foods aren’t just about eating. They’re one of the best ways to practice the fine motor skills she’ll need for writing, drawing, and self-feeding for the rest of her life.

The move

Offer small, soft pieces: peas, cooked carrot, banana chunks, small pasta. Let her pick them up herself even if it’s messy. The mess is the point.

Cruising and pre-walking

Cruising — walking while holding onto furniture — usually peaks around 9–12 months. Emma doesn’t need to be pushed to walk. What she needs is safe space to pull up, fall down safely, and build the confidence to let go.

The move

Clear furniture edges, put away sharp-cornered tables, and let her cruise freely. If she’s not cruising yet, check with your pediatrician at the 12-month visit — it’s worth mentioning.
