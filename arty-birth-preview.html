Heads up: 5 windows close this month — meaning the natural developmental timing is ending. These are worth doing first.

This month's priority

First pediatrician visit — 3 to 5 daysCloses in 1w

The 3–5 day visit is primarily about weight. Newborns lose up to 10% of their birth weight in the first days and should be back to birth weight by 10–14 days.

The move

* Schedule before discharge from the hospital

Newborn hearing screen — before hospital dischargeCloses in 1w

1–3 per 1,000 babies are born with permanent hearing loss. Early detection produces dramatically better language outcomes.

The move

* Confirm with the nursing staff that the hearing screen has been scheduled before discharge

Newborn metabolic / blood spot screen — hospitalCloses in 1w

The newborn blood spot screen tests for 30+ serious conditions including PKU, congenital hypothyroidism, and cystic fibrosis. Most are treatable when caught early.

The move

* The test is done in the hospital, usually 24–48 hours after birth

Newborn bilirubin (jaundice) checkCloses in 2w

Newborn jaundice affects 60% of term newborns. Most cases are benign but severe untreated jaundice can cause brain damage (kernicterus).

The move

* Confirm bilirubin was checked before hospital discharge

Safe sleep setup — before baby arrivesCloses in 4w

Approximately 3,500 infants die each year in the US from sleep-related causes. The vast majority are preventable with the right sleep environment.

The move

* Baby sleeps alone, on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in a crib, bassinet, or play yard that meets current safety standards

Get ready for next month

Introduce a bottle — breastfed babies only

Prep: If you're breastfeeding and want to introduce a bottle, 4 to 6 weeks is the recommended window — early enough that breastfeeding is established, late enough to avoid nipple confusion.

Social smile appears — first intentional interaction

Prep: Your baby's first intentional smile is coming — watch for it after a feed when they're content and looking at your face. It will stop you cold.

Respond to your baby's smile — the return volley

Prep: Smile back every time. That back-and-forth is wiring your baby's social brain and you cannot overdo it.

Head control — holds steady when upright

Prep: Keep up tummy time and begin carrying your baby upright against your shoulder — both build the neck strength for head control.
